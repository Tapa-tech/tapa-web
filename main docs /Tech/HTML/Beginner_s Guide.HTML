Ganesh Chaturthi for Beginners

No priest needed. No grand setup. Just clay, devotion, and this guide.

🙏No pandit needed 🏠Works in a small flat ⏱10 minutes a day ₹Under ₹500 to start ✓Nothing here can go wrong
JUMP TO 📋 The six steps 💭 Common worries 📖 What to read next

You want to bring Ganpati home. Here's everything you need to know.

You've seen the neighbours set up an altar. You've seen the pandals in your city. You've seen the visarjan processions. And this year, you want to bring Ganesha home — to your home.

But you've never done it before, and the whole thing feels intimidating. There seem to be rules. There seem to be things you could get wrong.

It isn't intimidating. And you can't really get it wrong.

◷ WHAT THIS ACTUALLY TAKES
TOTAL COST
₹100–500
A small clay idol. Everything else you likely have.
TIME PER DAY
10 minutes
Five in the morning, five in the evening.
HOW MANY DAYS
1½ to 10
Your choice. All are equally valid.
DO YOU NEED A PANDIT
No
This is a home practice. You are the worshipper.
+The six steps

Read them once. You will not need to come back mid-puja.

1
Buy a clay idol
In the weeks before

Go to any local market. Buy a small clay idol — shadu mati or natural clay. Size does not matter; six inches to twelve is perfect for a home.

Avoid Plaster of Paris if you can. Clay dissolves in water for visarjan. PoP does not.

₹100–500 for a small clay idol. That's it.
2
Set up the altar
The day before, or that morning

Clear a small table, stool or shelf. Cover it with a red or yellow cloth. Place the idol facing north or east. Set a small kalash — any brass or copper pot — with water and a coconut beside it.

No kalash? A glass of water with a tulsi leaf works fine.

You do not need a puja room. A clean corner of your living room is perfect. Ganesha is a guest — he sits where your family gathers, not in a locked room.
3
The sthapana
Day 1 · Monday, 14 September 2026

The best time is the Madhyahna muhurat, roughly 11:00 AM to 1:30 PM. If you miss it, the evening works too.

Place the idol on the altar. Say the mantra.

ॐ गं गणपतये नमः
Om Gan Ganpataye Namah
One mantra. Six syllables. This is genuinely enough.

Offer what you have — marigolds, modak or any sweet, a lit diya, incense. Twenty-one blades of durva grass if you can find them; that is his favourite offering.

Then do the aarti — Jai Ganesh Jai Ganesh Deva. Look it up on YouTube if you don't know the tune. Nobody is going to judge you.

You may read about prana pratishtha — the formal rite of inviting Ganesha's presence into the clay. It exists, and you can learn it later. Placing the idol with devotion and saying the mantra is already a complete act.
4
The daily routine
Day 2 onwards

Morning — fresh flowers, a sweet, light a diya, say the mantra. Five minutes.

Evening — gather whoever is home, light a diya, do the aarti. Five minutes.

That is the whole routine. Ten minutes a day. Consistency matters far more than elaboration — so once you have started, try not to skip a day.

5
How many days?
Decide before you start

Entirely your choice. These are the usual options, and all of them are equally valid:

1½ days 3 days 5 days 7 days 10 days

Ten days ends on Anant Chaturdashi, 23 September. If this is your first time, the two marked in pink are the easiest to keep.

There is no "better" duration. Nobody keeps score, and no tradition ranks a shorter observance below a longer one.
6
Visarjan — the farewell
On your chosen last day

Do one last puja — the best one you have done all week. Then gently lift the idol and take it to water.

If you have a river, pond or lake nearby — immerse the idol there.

If you are in a flat — fill a large clean bucket or tub with water. Place the idol in it. Let the clay dissolve overnight. In the morning, use that water for your plants, or pour it at the base of a tree. The clay returns to the earth. That is the visarjan. It is not a lesser version of one.

गणपति बाप्पा मोरया, पुढच्या वर्षी लवकर या
Ganpati Bappa Morya, pudhchya varshi lavkar ya — come back early next year.

That's it. You have done your first Ganesh Chaturthi at home.

+Common worries — answered

Every one of these has been asked by someone doing this for the first time.

?"I don't know the mantras."
→
Om Gan Ganpataye Namah is enough. One mantra, six syllables. Say it slowly and mean it.
?"I can't keep Ganpati for ten days."
→
A day and a half is equally valid. No one is ranking you, and no tradition ranks you either.
?"My apartment is too small."
→
A six-inch idol on a stool with a cloth over it is a complete altar. That is not a compromise version.
?"I don't have a river nearby for visarjan."
→
A bucket works. The ritual is the immersion — the return of clay to water — not the river.
?"Do I need a pandit?"
→
No. This is a home practice. You are the worshipper. Booking someone is a convenience, never a requirement.
?"What if I do something wrong?"
→
Ganesha is the most forgiving deity in the tradition. Approach with sincerity and that covers everything you are worried about.

Nobody is born knowing how to do this. The neighbours you watched setting up their altar learned it from someone, who learned it from someone else — and every one of them got something slightly wrong the first year, and it did not matter.

Buy the clay. Clear the table. Say the six syllables. The rest you will learn by doing it, the way everyone before you did.
