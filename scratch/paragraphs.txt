The Kanwar Yatra (pilgrimage to collect Ganga jal for Shivalinga abhishek) is a strong North Indian Pratha expression, not observed universally. Datura and bhang offerings are regional folk practices, not pan-Hindu textual requirements.

160: Intro Prose
161: ↳ Evergreen — no specific date or year in the body. Dates live in the Panchang Card only. The Samudra Manthan opening sets the WHY immediately.
162: Sawan Somwar Vrat is the Monday fast of the month of Shravan — and according to the Shiva Purana and multiple other named texts, it is among the most meritorious vrats a devotee can observe in a year. The month of Shravan belongs to Shiva in a way no other month does. The fasts that fall within it — one Monday at a time — carry the weight of that relationship.
163: What circulates about Sawan Somwar online is a mix of genuine scriptural grounding and popular embellishment: conflicting fasting rules, fear about what happens if you miss a Monday, and confusion about which dates apply in which part of India. This guide cuts through all of it.

171: Section: Why Shravan Belongs to Shiva
172: ↳ Two narratives — the Samudra Manthan (Puranic) and Parvati's tapasya (also Puranic, as referenced in the Shiva Purana). Both are Dharma. Samudra Manthan is the stronger textual foundation for why we pour water specifically.
173: Narrative 1 of 2
174: Samudra Manthan — why water is poured on Shiva in Shravan
175: The Bhagavata Purana and Vishnu Purana both describe the Samudra Manthan — the churning of the cosmic ocean by the devas and asuras, who sought the amrit (nectar of immortality) hidden within it. When the ocean yielded its contents, not everything that surfaced was nourishing. The halahala — a poison of such force that it threatened to destroy all of creation — rose first. It was Shiva who stepped forward and drank it, holding it in his throat rather than swallowing it. His throat turned blue from the poison; he is known ever since as Neelkanth.
176: The churning is said to have happened during the month of Shravan. After Shiva drank the halahala, the devas poured water and milk over him continuously through the month — to cool the burning in his throat and honour the sacrifice he had made. What we do on every Sawan Monday is a continuation of that act: we pour water, milk, and panchamrit over the Shivalinga in gratitude and reverence. Not a ritual of fear, but of acknowledgment.

178: Narrative 2 of 2
179: Monday as Shiva's day — and why Shravan Mondays multiply the merit
180: The Shiva Purana explains the connection between Monday and Shiva through the story of Chandra, the Moon. Chandra carries the nectar of the gods and was always closely associated with Shiva — who wears the crescent moon in his matted hair as an ornament. This association makes Monday — Somwar, the day of Soma / Chandra — sacred to Shiva in the same way Friday belongs to Lakshmi or Saturday to Shani.
181: A Monday vrat in any month carries merit. But when Monday falls inside Shravan — the month already connected to Shiva through the Samudra Manthan — the Shiva Purana describes those days as particularly auspicious for sincere worship. The Vidyeshvara Samhita section explicitly recommends the Shravan Somvar vrat for unmarried persons seeking a worthy partner, for married couples seeking harmony, and for any devotee carrying a heartfelt sankalp.

243: Section: The Puja
244: ↳ 7 steps. The Sankalp step is distinctive to this article — not in Nag Panchami — and should be kept exactly as written. Mantra chanting in step 5 uses two specific texts; do not paraphrase or reduce.
245: 1
246: Rise before sunrise — Brahma Muhurta
247: Bathe in cool water. A few drops of Ganga Jal added to the bath is the practice of many households. Wear clean clothes — preferably white or saffron, though any clean clothes are appropriate.
248: [Dharma]
249: 2
250: Take the Vrat Sankalp
251: Sit facing east before the Shivalinga or your home shrine. Take a small spoon of water in your right palm and make a quiet resolve to keep this vrat with sincerity. State the Monday count — first, second, third, or fourth Sawan Somwar.
252: [Dharma]
253: 3
254: Perform Jalabhishek
255: Pour water slowly and steadily over the Shivalinga — Ganga Jal first if available, then cow's milk, then panchamrit (curd, ghee, honey, sugar in milk) if observing the full form. End with clean water. This is the central act of the day.
256: [Dharma — core act]
257: 4
258: Offer bilva patra, flowers, and akshata
259: Lay bilva leaves on the linga with their stem pointing toward you. Offer any white or saffron flowers. Scatter akshata (turmeric-stained rice) around the base.
260: [Dharma]  [Flower colour — Pratha]
261: 5
262: Chant Om Namah Shivaya and the Mahamrityunjaya Mantra
263: Om Namah Shivaya (the Panchakshara mantra of the Krishna Yajurveda, Shri Rudram) — chant 11, 21, 51, or 108 times with a calm mind and a rudraksha mala if available. The Mahamrityunjaya Mantra (Rigveda 7.59.12) may be chanted additionally for 108 repetitions.
264: [Dharma]  [Krishna Yajurveda (Shri Rudram)]
265: 6
266: Listen to or read the Vrat Katha
267: The Shiva Purana katha — the story of the merchant's son — is the narrative vehicle for the merit of this vrat. Read or listen sincerely. If observing Solah Somvar, include the full katha of the 16-Monday vow.
268: [Dharma]  [Shiva Purana]
269: 7
270: Perform aarti and break the fast
271: Light the diya and perform Shiva aarti. Break the fast after the evening puja, ideally after sunset, with sattvic food — fruits, milk, vrat-friendly foods. Avoid grains, lentils, regular salt, onion, and garlic for the full day.
272: [Dharma]
273: Fasting — three accepted forms
274: Nirjala — no food or water. Demanding; only for those physically suited.     |     Phalahar — fruits, milk, sattvic vrat foods. Widely practised and fully accepted.     |     Sajal / one sattvic meal — permitted where health requires it.
275: The Shiva Purana prescribes devotion and the sankalp, not a specific fast form. Phalahar and sajal are explicitly accepted across the tradition. Difficulty is not what is being asked for.
276: Section: Solah Somvar — What It Is and Who It's For
277: ↳ Distinct from Sawan Somwar (4 Mondays). Solah Somvar = 16 consecutive Mondays, typically begun on the first Sawan Monday. Explain the difference clearly so users don't conflate the two.
278: Many people first encounter Sawan Somwar through Solah Somvar — the vow of sixteen consecutive Mondays dedicated to Shiva. The two are related but distinct.
279: Sawan Somwar Vrat is the observance of the four (or five) Mondays that fall within the month of Shravan. It is a contained, seasonal practice — it begins with the first Sawan Monday and ends with the last.
280: Solah Somvar is a longer, independent vow: sixteen consecutive Mondays, beginning on any Monday (ideally the first Sawan Somwar) and continuing for sixteen weeks regardless of month boundaries. The Shiva Purana katha that accompanies Solah Somvar — the story of the merchant's son and the young girl who kept the fast to find a husband like Shiva — is among the most widely known Shiva kathas in North India. The udyapan (concluding rite) is performed on the seventeenth Monday.
281: [Dharma · 4/5]  [Shiva Purana]
282: Both vrats are valid. If your intention is seasonal devotion within Shravan, observe the four Sawan Mondays. If your sankalp extends beyond the month — for a specific wish, for a partner, for family harmony — Solah Somvar is the appropriate form. Begin on the first Sawan Monday; if you miss one by circumstance, add one extra Monday at the end. The tradition makes this provision explicitly; a missed Monday does not invalidate what came before.

291: Section: Myths &amp; Facts
292: ↳ Three myth cards. All three directly contradict real Puranic guidance — cite the textual source in each fact correction.
293: ✕  &quot;If you miss one Sawan Monday, all the previous Mondays' vrats are lost.&quot;
294: ✓  No text makes this claim. The tradition provides for exactly this situation: if you miss a Monday by circumstance, add one Monday at the end to keep the count unbroken. The vrat is a sankalp of devotion — the tradition is not punitive about human circumstance.
295: ✕  &quot;Only a pandit can perform Rudrabhishek — doing it yourself doesn't count.&quot;
296: ✓  Any sincere devotee can pour water and milk on the Shivalinga and chant Om Namah Shivaya. The Shiva Purana does not restrict jalabhishek to priests. A pandit adds mantra expertise and pace for a larger gathering — not validity to the worship.
297: ✕  &quot;The Sawan Somwar fast must be nirjala — no food or water — or it has no merit.&quot;
298: ✓  No Puranic text mandates the waterless form for Sawan Somwar. Phalahar (fruits and milk) and even a single sattvic meal are accepted forms of the vrat across the tradition. The Shiva Purana prescribes devotion and the sankalp — not endurance. A phalahar fast observed with full sincerity is a complete vrat.
299: Closing Prose
300: ↳ 1–2 paragraphs. The tone of this close should echo the Neelkanth image — Shiva absorbing difficulty so others don't have to. The vrat is an act of acknowledgment and reciprocity, not transaction.
301: Shiva drank poison so the world wouldn't have to. Every Sawan Monday, when you pour water over the Shivalinga, you are repeating what the devas did after the Manthan — acknowledging a sacrifice made for you, cooling what burned, expressing gratitude in the only way available: with water, with devotion, with your presence.
302: That is the tradition. Not a schedule of requirements. Not fear of what happens if you miss a day. A living acknowledgment between a devotee and a deity who has already shown, in the oldest story we have, what it looks like to absorb the worst and hold it so others can live.
