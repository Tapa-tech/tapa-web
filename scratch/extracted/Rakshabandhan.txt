Raksha Bandhan: The Raksha Sutra, the Mantra, and the Three Puranic Narratives
Wife, friend, devotee — the thread protects whoever you tie it on

Intro
The Bhavishya Purana does not describe a sister tying a decorative thread on her brother's wrist and receiving a gift in return. It describes something older and wider: a sacred thread, consecrated with a specific mantra, tied on the wrist of someone you want to protect — and it worked. The thread itself carried the power of the prayer. It was a shield.
In the founding narrative, it is a wife who ties it on her husband. In the Mahabharata, a friend ties it on a friend. In the Bhagavata Purana, a devotee ties it on a king. The common element is not the relationship — it is the thread, the mantra, and the intention.

The Raksha Sutra Mantra
येन बद्धो बली राजा दानवेन्द्रो महाबलः
तेन त्वामभिबध्नामि रक्षे मा चल मा चल
'With the same bond that held King Bali, I bind you. O protector, be steadfast — do not falter.'
[Dharma · 4/5]  [Bhavishya Purana, Uttara Parva Ch.137]
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Three Narratives
1. SACHI-INDRA (Wife to Husband, Bhavishya Purana): Indra losing a war. Sachi ties consecrated thread on his wrist. He wins.
2. DRAUPADI-KRISHNA (Friend to Friend, Mahabharata): Draupadi tears saree to bind Krishna's wound. He reciprocates during Vastraaharan.
3. LAKSHMI-BALI (Devotee to King, Bhagavata Purana): Lakshmi ties thread on Bali's wrist to free Vishnu from doorkeeper duty.
None are sibling relationships. The thread is the constant.

The Vidhi
①  Prepare thali: Raksha Sutra, kumkum, akshat, diya, sweets.
②  Seat the person receiving the thread, facing east.
③  Apply tilak — kumkum and akshat on forehead.
④  Tie the Raksha Sutra on the right wrist while reciting the mantra.
⑤  Perform aarti — wave diya clockwise.
⑥  Feed sweets and receive blessings.
No fasting. Not a fasting festival. The ritual is the tying.

Dharma vs Pratha
DHARMA: The mantra (Bhavishya Purana). The three narratives. The consecrated thread concept.
PRATHA: Sibling-specific form. Decorative rakhis. Gift exchange. 'The gift proves the love.' All culturally dominant — not Puranic.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Myths & Facts
✕  "Raksha Bandhan is ONLY between siblings."
✓  The three founding narratives are wife-husband, friend-friend, devotee-king. None are siblings.
✕  "The gift proves the love."
✓  No Puranic text names a gift. The thread IS the complete ritual act.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Closing
The word 'rakshe' — protector — is the root of the festival's name. Not 'gift.' Not 'sibling.' Protector. That is what the thread becomes when the mantra is spoken.
