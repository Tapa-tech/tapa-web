A1.  Source-of-Truth Panel
  ↳ Hartalika Teej has a specific Puranic episode - the friend who abducted Parvati - which is distinct from both Hariyali Teej (the tapasya narrative) and Kajari Teej (folk tradition). The founding episode is in the Shiva Purana.

FIELD
WHAT IT SHOWS
Primary Source
Shiva Purana (Rudra Samhita / Parvati Khanda) - the Hartalika episode: Parvati's friend takes her into the forest to prevent an unwanted marriage, enabling her to complete her tapasya for Shiva. This is a named, specific episode within the broader Parvati narrative. The name 'Hartalika' itself derives from this episode: 'harat' (abduction) + 'aalika' (female friend). Corroborated in Skanda Purana.
Regional Variance
Most prominent in UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand. Also observed in Nepal. In Gujarat known as Kevda Trij (Kevada Teej). In Karnataka/Andhra as Gowri Habba (with some ritual variation). Not a pan-Hindu observance - largely absent from South Indian Tamil and Kerala calendars, and from Bengal.
Optional Elements
The nirjala (waterless) form of the fast is the most widely observed for this Teej - unlike Hariyali and Kajari Teej where it is optional. The sajal (with water) form is accepted for health reasons but nirjala is the dominant tradition. Sand/clay idol-making, night jagaran, solah shringar, green/red attire, mehndi - all Pratha elements. The core is: fasting + Shiva-Parvati worship + katha recitation + idol immersion next morning.
Confidence Score
4 out of 5 - specific Puranic episode (the Hartalika incident) in the Shiva Purana. Stronger founding narrative than Kajari Teej (folk, 3/5); comparable to Hariyali Teej (also Shiva Purana, 4/5) but with a distinct named episode.

Intro Prose
  ↳ Evergreen. No year or date in body. Open with the name etymology - 'hartalika' = abduction by a female friend - because the name IS the story. Position it immediately as the strictest of the three Teejs.

The name tells you what happened. Hartalika is a compound of two words: harat, meaning to take away, and aalika, meaning a female friend. On this day, Parvati's closest friend took her - abducted her, in the language of the text - deep into a forest so that she could worship Shiva undisturbed, instead of being married off to someone she did not choose.
Hartalika Teej falls on the third day (Tritiya) of Shukla Paksha in the month of Bhadrapada. It is the strictest of the three Teej festivals: a 24-hour nirjala fast, an all-night vigil, and the making and immersion of sand or clay idols of Shiva and Parvati - all of which mirror, in practice, what Parvati herself is said to have done on this night.
Where Hariyali Teej (Shravana Shukla Tritiya) celebrates the monsoon's arrival and Parvati's tapasya in general, and Kajari Teej (Bhadrapada Krishna Tritiya) is carried by folk tradition and the kajri songs, Hartalika Teej is anchored in a specific Puranic episode - the friend's intervention - that gives the festival its name, its narrative, and its distinctive practices.

Section: The Hartalika Episode - Why the Friend Matters
  ↳ One narrative - the Hartalika episode from the Shiva Purana. This is the founding story. The friend's act is not just backdrop - it is the festival's name, its identity, and its philosophical point: that devotion sometimes requires someone who helps you get to where you need to be.

Narrative 1 of 1
The Hartalika Episode - Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita / Parvati Khanda
In her second birth, Parvati - the daughter of Himavan, king of the mountains - carried the same sankalp from her life as Sati: that Shiva and no one else would be her husband. But Shiva was deep in meditation, withdrawn from the world. And Himavan, like Daksha before him, had other plans.
When the sage Narada arrived at Himavan's court and proposed that Parvati be given in marriage to Lord Vishnu, Himavan accepted. It was a reasonable, honourable match. But it was not the match Parvati had resolved upon.
Parvati confided in her closest friend - a sakhi whose name the texts do not give, but whose action they remember. The friend listened, understood, and acted. She took Parvati away from the palace - harat, an abduction - deep into a dense forest where no one would find them. There, in the shelter of the trees, beside a river, Parvati shaped a Shivalinga from the sand of the riverbed.
She worshipped it through the entire night of Bhadrapada Shukla Tritiya. No food. No water. No sleep. Only the sand idol, the forest, the river, and the resolve she had carried across two lifetimes.
At dawn, Shiva appeared. Moved by the depth of her tapasya and the sincerity of her devotion - sustained not by circumstance but by choice, enabled not by her father but by a friend - he accepted her. He told her: any woman who fasts on this day, makes an idol from sand, worships me through the night, and hears this story will receive what you have received.
Parvati returned to Himavan. The marriage was performed - to Shiva. The friend who had enabled it returned too, unnamed but not unremembered. The festival that carries her act in its name is the remembrance.

Section: The Vidhi - Step by Step
  ↳ 8 steps spanning evening of 13 Sep through morning of 15 Sep. The two-night structure is distinctive: puja begins evening of the 13th (Dwitiya), the full fast and jagaran run through the 14th (Tritiya), and fast breaks morning of the 15th. Make this structure explicit.

Hartalika Teej spans two evenings and the night between them. The puja setup begins on the evening of Bhadrapada Shukla Dwitiya (13 September). The full fast and jagaran run through Tritiya (14 September). The fast breaks and idol immersion happen the morning of Chaturthi (15 September).
1
Bathe and dress in green or red - evening of 13 September
Bathe in the evening. Wear clean traditional attire - green is most common, red is equally traditional. Apply mehndi if this is your practice. The shringar is part of the saubhagya observance.
 [Dharma - preparatory]  [Colour and shringar - Pratha] 
2
Make sand or clay idols of Shiva, Parvati, and Ganesha
Using clean river sand or clay, shape three idols: a Shivalinga, a figure of Parvati, and a figure of Ganesha. Place them on a banana leaf or peepal leaf on a clean surface. This is not decorative - Parvati herself made a Shivalinga from sand in the Puranic narrative.
 [Dharma - core act]  [Shiva Purana] 
3
Take the Vrat Sankalp
Sit before the idols facing east. Take water in your right palm and resolve: to observe this vrat with sincerity through the night until morning, for the wellbeing of your marriage and family, or for the sankalp you carry.
 [Dharma] 
4
Perform abhishek and puja of the sand idol
Pour water (Ganga Jal if available), then milk, over the Shivalinga. Offer belpatra, dhatura, and white flowers to Shiva. Offer sindoor, kumkum, and red flowers to Parvati. Place akshat and fruits before Ganesha. Light the diya and incense.
 [Dharma - core puja] 
5
Listen to or recite the Vrat Katha
The katha - Shiva narrating the Hartalika episode: Parvati's friend taking her to the forest, the night of tapasya, the sand Shivalinga, Shiva's acceptance - is recited in the evening in the presence of the idols. This is the essential narrative act. Read it aloud or have it read while you listen.
 [Dharma - mandatory]  [Shiva Purana] 
6
Stay awake through the night - jagaran
The night vigil is the Pratha heart of Hartalika Teej. Stay awake through the night with bhajans, Teej songs, and devotional reading. Many women observe jagaran in groups. The diya should remain lit through the night. If you doze briefly from exhaustion, this does not invalidate the vrat.
 [Pratha - strongly traditional] 
7
Morning puja and aarti - 15 September
At dawn on the morning of the 15th, perform a final aarti of the sand idols. Offer fresh flowers. Chant the Shanti Mantra.
 [Dharma] 
8
Immerse the idols and break the fast
Carry the sand/clay idols to a river, pond, or water body and immerse them. Return home. Bathe. Break the fast with water, then fruits, then a light meal. The fast is now complete.
 [Dharma - ritual completion]

Section: Myths & Facts
  ↳ Three myth cards. The Hariyali/Hartalika confusion is the most important - it is in every Teej article and in the Three Teejs Comparison. Keep the language consistent across all documents.

✕  "If you don't keep a nirjala fast, the Hartalika Teej vrat doesn't count at all."
✓  The Shiva Purana prescribes fasting with devotion on this day - it does not mandate the waterless form specifically. Nirjala is the dominant Pratha for Hartalika Teej and is observed by the large majority of women, but sajal and phalahar are accepted forms, especially for pregnant women, the elderly, or anyone with a health condition. A fast adapted to your capacity, kept with sincerity, is a complete vrat.

✕  "If you fall asleep during the jagaran, the entire vrat is wasted and must be repeated."
✓  No text states this. The jagaran is a strongly observed Pratha - it is central to the experience of Hartalika Teej and to the community gathering that defines it. But dozing briefly from exhaustion after hours of devotion does not reset the vrat's merit. The tradition asks for sincerity sustained through a long night, not for a physical endurance test.

✕  "Hartalika Teej and Hariyali Teej are the same festival observed at different times."
✓  They are two distinct festivals. Hartalika Teej falls on Bhadrapada Shukla Tritiya (14 September in 2026) and takes its name from a specific Puranic episode - the friend who took Parvati into the forest. Hariyali Teej falls on Shravana Shukla Tritiya (August) and takes its name from hariyali - the greenery of the monsoon. Different month, different founding narrative, different name etymology, and a different fast-breaking condition: Hartalika breaks the next morning after 24 hours; Hariyali breaks at moonrise or the next morning depending on family tradition.

Closing Prose
  ↳ 1-2 paragraphs. The arc: the friend. Hartalika Teej is the only festival in the Hindu calendar named for a friend's act. The friend is unnamed but her action is what made the devotion possible.

The unnamed friend is the quiet centre of this festival. She is not a goddess. She is not a sage. She does not perform tapasya or receive a boon. She listens to what Parvati wants, understands that Parvati cannot get it without help, and acts - taking her friend away from a palace and a father's plans, into a forest where devotion can happen undisturbed.
That is what the name remembers. Not the tapasya. Not the boon. Not the marriage. The friend. Hartalika Teej asks its observers to bring the same quality into their own devotion: not just resolve, but the willingness to act on behalf of someone else's resolve. To make the sand idol with your own hands, to stay awake through the night, to carry the idol to the river at dawn - and to remember that none of it would have happened without someone who said: come, I will take you where you need to be.
